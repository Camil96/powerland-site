How does a PV installation work for businesses?

A PV installation consists of solar panels, inverters, mounting structures and monitoring. The system converts sunlight into electricity that is used directly in your business. Surplus energy can be stored in a battery or used to charge electric vehicles.

Produce:

  • when sunlight is available
  • based on kWp capacity
  • with real-time monitoring

Use / Store:

  • direct consumption
  • battery storage
  • EV charging infrastructure

Possible configurations for solar panels

Roof installations for warehouses and offices

  • High-efficiency panels tailored to load capacity and orientation, including shadow analysis
  • Suitable for large roof surfaces with maximum yield
  • Ideal for direct self-consumption during working hours

Ground-mounted systems on open land

  • Ideal when roof space is limited or unsuitable
  • Stable steel structures on open areas or industrial plots
  • Perfect to combine with battery storage and charging plazas

Solar carports and canopies for car parks

  • Energy generation and vehicle protection in one structure
  • Suitable for retail, logistics, fleet and leasing companies and businesses with large employee vehicle fleets
  • Ideal to combine with charging infrastructure for public or private charging

What is the investment and payback period?

Prices vary depending on roof type, cabling, inverters and any integration with batteries or charging stations.

ROI-highlights:

  • payback period: 3–7 years
  • higher ROI for daytime consumption profiles
  • ideal combination with battery storage

What is included in a professional PV installation?

  • High-efficiency solar panels (mono / bifacial)

Without storage or smart control, the average business uses only 20 to 40% of its own solar production. The rest is fed back into the grid.

Questions about
solar panels

For businesses, we always choose monocrystalline panels (high efficiency) or bifacial panels (yield on both sides). Depending on shade and layout we use string inverters or optimizers.

The feed-in compensation for businesses is low, so optimizing self-consumption is more important than injecting power. With battery storage and smart control, injection can be greatly limited. If you do not install a battery, we dimension the PV installation consciously smaller, so that it matches your daily profile as much as possible and as little energy as possible is lost via the grid.

With a dynamic energy contract, your electricity price follows the market (hourly tariffs or quarter-hour prices). A PV installation reduces your consumption during expensive hours, while a battery stores power when the price is low. With EMS control, Powerland automates this process: charging at low tariffs, discharging at expensive hours. This maximizes your saving.
